# Project Showcase Rulebook - IIUC Computer Club
<!-- p.3 -->
## Theme
Build. Integrate. Inspire. Where software, hardware and hybrid innovation come to life.
<!-- p.4 -->
## II. Project Categories
Projects may be submitted under one of three categories: software projects, hardware projects, or hybrid projects.
<!-- p.5 -->
## III. Event Tracks
- Track 01, Software: web applications; mobile applications; AI/ML solutions; security systems; cloud-based systems; blockchain and decentralized systems.
- Track 02, Hardware: embedded systems; robotics; IoT devices; smart electronics; sensor-based solutions.
- Track 03, Hybrid: integration of software and hardware components; smart automation systems; AI-enabled IoT solutions; cyber-physical systems; 4th Industrial Revolution (4IR) systems.
<!-- p.6 -->
## IV. Eligibility
1. Participation is open to all undergraduate students of the CSE department.
2. Teams may consist of 3-4 members.
3. A student may participate in only one project team.
4. Inter-section teams are allowed. Students from different sections of the same batch or different batches may form a team.
5. Faculty members may act only as advisors and cannot participate in presentations.
6. Teams must designate one member as the Team Leader for all official communications.
7. Changes to team composition after the registration deadline will require approval from the organizing committee.
<!-- p.7 -->
## V. Registration Requirements
Each team must submit: team information; gender; category (software, hardware or hybrid); team member list; supervisor/advisor information (if applicable); registration fee.
<!-- p.8 -->
## VI. Project Requirements
- Originality: projects must be the team's original work. Previously submitted projects are allowed only if significant improvements have been made. Plagiarism will result in immediate disqualification.
- Development: projects must demonstrate substantial technical contribution. Use of existing frameworks, APIs and libraries is permitted. Teams must clearly explain their own contributions.
- Functionality: a working prototype/demo is highly encouraged. Simulations may be accepted if hardware implementation is not feasible.
<!-- p.9 -->
## VII. Hardware Requirements
Every hardware or hybrid project must include a physical prototype, working model, or demonstrable hardware subsystem. Projects may use Arduino, ESP32/ESP8266, Raspberry Pi, STM32, FPGA platforms, PLC systems, or similar devices. Teams must demonstrate understanding of circuit design, hardware architecture, component selection, communication protocols and power management.
<!-- p.10 -->
## VIII. Judging Criteria
Five criteria, 20 marks each, total 100: Genuineness & Originality; Innovation & Creativity; Technical Excellence; Functionality & Impact; Presentation & Defense.
<!-- p.11 -->
## IX. Project Showcase Judging Rubric
Each criterion is scored on four levels: Excellent (9-10), Good (7-8), Fair (4-6), Poor (0-3), with 20 marks per criterion and 100 in total. Excellent originality means significant original work where the team clearly explains all components, design decisions and implementation details. Excellent presentation means a clear, professional, engaging presentation with an excellent demonstration, and confident, accurate answers to all questions.
<!-- p.13 -->
## X. Disqualification Rules
A team may be disqualified for: plagiarism or copyright violation; false claims regarding project development; failure to demonstrate major claimed functionalities; offensive or unethical content, or unsafe electrical or mechanical designs; failure to appear during the scheduled presentation; misconduct toward judges, organizers, or participants.
<!-- p.14 -->
## XI. Project Submission and Presentation Guidelines
Each team must submit these items before the specified deadline: a project report, and a recorded video presentation.
<!-- p.15 -->
Project report guidelines: a detailed project report in PDF format, recommended length 5-15 pages. It should include title and team information; abstract; problem statement; objectives; methodology/system design/hardware architecture (block diagram, system architecture diagram); components list (bill of materials), sensor and actuator integration and controller programming overview for hardware and hybrid projects; implementation details; results and evaluation; future scope; references.
<!-- p.16 -->
Recorded video presentation guidelines: duration up to 5 minutes. The video should clearly demonstrate the problem being addressed, key features of the solution, the system architecture/design, and a working demonstration of the project. Accepted formats: MP4, AVI, or MOV.
<!-- p.17 -->
## XII. Submission Method
Both the project report and the recorded video must be uploaded to the designated Google Drive link provided by the organizers. Teams are responsible for ensuring successful upload before the deadline. Late submissions may result in penalties or disqualification.
<!-- p.18 -->
## XIII. Showcase Presentation Format
During the Project Showcase, teams may present using one or more of these formats: poster presentation; working prototype (strongly encouraged for hardware and hybrid projects); software application demonstration; slide presentation; hybrid demonstration (software and hardware). Teams are encouraged to use the format that best represents their project.
<!-- p.19 -->
## XIV. Safety Regulations
Hazardous chemicals, explosives, exposed high-voltage circuits, unsafe battery configurations and any dangerous designs are prohibited. All electrical connections must be properly insulated and secured. The organizing committee may stop demonstrations of unsafe projects.
